ST Maui
vigorous tree – round shape -  resistant to anthracnose – aggressive  growing - needs pruning – taste like guava.

The ST Maui mango is a rare, highly prized variety originally from Hawaii. In the 1990s, the budwood was sent to Florida, where it is now more widely grown than in its home state. It is known for its sweet, fiber-free flesh and a unique flavor often described as a mix of guava, fruit punch, and passion fruit
 
As noted, it is verified by horticultural records that "ST" represents a specific person's initials from the University of Hawaii evaluation program. It is a common misconception among casual buyers that it stands for "Saint" due to how it's often spoken aloud.
 
Sweet Tart
The Sweet Tart mango is a highly prized mango developed in Boynton Beach, Florida. It gets its name from its signature flavor, which tastes like sweet-and-sour candy. It is a mid-season variety that produces fiberless, deep orange flesh and ripens between July and August. 

Tree Size: They are vigorous, upright growers that can eventually reach 20-30 feet tall. Will need runing.
The tree shows excellent resistance to anthracnose

The Sweet Tart Mango takes your taste buds on a ride throughout its every level of ripeness. For the highest tart intensity, give it a whirl when it's slightly under ripe and mostly green.

Orange Sherbet
Fruit Profile & Flavor
·       The Taste: True to its name, the flavor is intensely sweet and rich with a distinct, complex, orange-citrus candy profile. Many growers and fruit enthusiasts describe the experience as tasting exactly like an orange sherbet push-pop or an orange creamsicle. It has a sharp, refreshing citrus zest that cuts through its intense sweetness
Tree Characteristics & Growth Habit
·       Size & Manageability: The tree exhibits moderate vigor. While some nurseries categorize its growth as compact or semi-dwarf, it can easily be maintained via annual pruning to a highly manageable "pickable" backyard height of 10 to 15 feet.
·       Production: It is a regular and heavy mid-season bearer, typically ripening in June and July in Florida. It has a tendency to produce fruit in heavy clusters.
·       Disease Resistance: One of its biggest advantages over its parent ('Lemon Meringue') and sibling ('Lemon Zest') is its superior resistance to Bacterial Black Spot and anthracnose, making it a much more resilient choice for Florida’s humid climate.
·       Seed Type: The seed is small and polyembryonic, meaning seedlings will frequently grow true-to-type clones of the mother tree (though purchasing a grafted tree is always recommended for predictable, faster fruiting).
 
 
Peach Cobbler
 
The Peach Cobbler mango is one of the more intriguing modern cultivars from Gary Zill's breeding program in Boynton Beach, Florida. Despite its name, many experienced tasters say it doesn't actually taste like peach cobbler. Instead, it has a rich, complex citrus flavor with subtle spice and peach-like undertones. It is considered a "collector's mango" because of its outstanding eating quality, although it has some growing quirks.
 
Flavor
·  Exceptionally rich and sweet
·  Notes of sweet tangerine and citrus
·  Hints of warm baking spices such as cinnamon
·  Completely fiberless
 
Tree characteristics
The tree is:
Vigorous
Dense and spreading
Medium to large if left unpruned
Slow to begin fruiting (not very precocious)
When it was first evaluated, Peach Cobbler trees were slow to flower and set only moderate crops. As the trees matured, however, flowering and production improved noticeably
Guava
he "Wow" Factor
Unique Aroma & Identity: Originally known as 'Sein Ta Lone' ("Diamond Solitaire") in Myanmar, it earned the name "Guava" because its intense, perfume-like aroma closely mimics a freshly picked, ripe pink guava.
Visual Appeal: Produces striking, bright-yellow fruit that often develops a soft pink/rose blush on the skin and flesh.
Top-Tier Dessert Quality: Highly prized among collectors for delivering an exotic tropical taste without the resinous or medicinal notes found in some traditional varieties.
Flavor & Texture Profile
Flavor: Intensely sweet and rich with a distinct rosé wine compilation flavor—layered with floral notes and an undeniable ripe guava zing built over a sweet mango base.
Texture: Silky, smooth, and 100% fiber-free with a melt-in-your-mouth feel.
Aroma: Extremely fragrant; the room fills with a musky, tropical, guava-floral scent when fully ripe.
Tree Characteristics & Disease Resistance
Growth Habit: Moderate to semi-dwarf growth habit with an open, spreading canopy. It is manageable, responds exceptionally well to post-harvest pruning, and can be maintained in small yards or containers.
Production: A reliable, mid-season bearer (typically harvesting May through July) known for producing heavy blooms and abundant fruit.
Disease Resistance:
Anthracnose: Shows moderate tolerance. While not completely immune, it handles humid conditions reasonably well with good canopy airflow and sanitation.
Bacterial Black Spot: Highly resistant.

White Piri
The "Wow" Factor
Complex Spice & Vanilla Notes: Originated in Hawaii and grown by rare fruit collectors in Florida, 'White Pirie' stands out for its unique Indian-spice flavor complexity. It delivers distinct warm spice tones and hints of vanilla rather than typical classic sweet or resinous mango profiles.
Flavor & Texture Profile
Flavor: Exceptionally rich and complex. It features a balanced blend of intense sweetness and bright acidity, underpinned by subtle notes of vanilla, warm spice, and floral aromatics.
Texture: Completely fiberless, soft, and juicy with a smooth, melt-in-your-mouth flesh.
Appearance: Medium-sized, rounded to slightly ovate fruit that turns vibrant yellow with a soft red blush as it ripens.
Tree Characteristics & Disease Resistance
Growth Habit: A slow-to-moderate grower with a dense, compact canopy. Because of its slow growth and manageable structure, it can easily be kept controlled in smaller landscapes.
Fruiting Season: Typically mid-season, ripening around July in Florida.
Disease Resistance:
Anthracnose & Fungi: Low resistance. 'White Pirie' is noticeably susceptible to fungal disease (anthracnose and powdery mildew) in humid climates.
Growing Tip: Because of its susceptibility to wet-weather fungal issues, it requires proactive pruning for air circulation, good sanitation, or planting in drier microclimates (like coastal areas) to thrive in Florida.


Mekony
"Wow" Factor
A Rare Historical Hybrid: Bred in West Palm Beach, Florida by renowned pomologist David Sturrock, 'Mekong' (often spelled Mekony) is a rare cross between an Indian-style cultivar ('Edward') and an Indochinese-style seedling.
Ultra-Exclusive: The variety is so uncommon that the original parent tree residing at Tropical Acres Farms in West Palm Beach is believed to be one of the only fruiting specimens in existence. This is where your fruit was bought
Distinct Fusion Profile: It stands out because it bridges two distinct mango worlds—combining the smooth, classic Indochinese/Thai eating quality with the spicy, aromatic punch of an Indian mango.
Flavor & Texture Profile
Flavor: Uniquely complex. The initial taste offers the clean, sweet, and bright floral notes of a traditional Thai mango, followed by a rich, resinous Indian mango component on the finish.
Texture: Orange, firm flesh that is 100% fiberless, making for a clean, smooth slice.
Tree Characteristics & Disease Resistance
Growth Habit: A large, historic tree with an upright, vertically oriented growth habit and a very dense canopy.
Fruiting Season & Seed: Mid-season bearer (typically harvesting late June through July in Florida) that produces polyembryonic seeds. Poly embryonic seeds produce a bunch of genetic clone seedlings and odd ball, the heterozygous one.
Disease Resistance:
Anthracnose: Highly resistant. The fruit holds up remarkably well against Florida’s summer rains and humidity without significant fungal spotting.
Bacterial Black Spot (BBS): Highly resistant.
Overall Hardiness: Exceptionally low-maintenance regarding fruit quality, consistently producing clean, disease-free crops year after year.


Lemon Meringue
The "Wow" Factor
The Original "Citrus-Flavor" Pioneer: Introduced from Myanmar (where it was called 'Po Pyu Kalay' or 'PPK'), 'Lemon Meringue' kickstarted Florida's obsession with citrus-flavored mango cultivars.
Sweet Lemon-Zest Punch: It delivers an intense, unmistakable flavor profile that legitimately tastes like sweet lemon candy or lemon meringue pie filling.
Small Polyembryonic Seed: The seed is very small, leaving a high flesh-to-seed ratio, and it produces polyembryonic clones.
Flavor & Texture Profile
Flavor: Bright, explosive, and zesty. It features a strong citrus-dominated profile—balancing intense sugary sweetness with tart, lemon-zest undertones and a rich tropical aroma. (Note: Heavy summer rains right at harvest can slightly dilute its intense citrus punch).
Texture: Pale-yellow to light-yellow flesh that is 100% fiberless, soft, juicy, and creamy.
Appearance: Small to medium, elongated oval fruit that ripens to a vibrant golden yellow.
Concentrated Citrus Oils: The peel contains a high concentration of aromatic essential oils and terpene compounds. Biting into or chewing near the skin gives an intense, sharp hit of tart lemon zest—much like biting into a lemon peel—which adds a bitter-tangy contrast to the sweet, pie-like flesh.
Edibility & Urushiol Warning: While mango skin is technically edible and packed with fiber and antioxidants, caution is necessary, because it also contains Urushiol, which occurs in poison ivy. If you are known to have a severe reaction to poison ivy, skip eating the skin.
Tree Characteristics & Disease Resistance
Growth Habit: Vigorous, upright, and vertical grower with dense foliage. Because of its vigorous nature, regular annual pruning is essential to manage its size and keep the canopy manageable.
Fruiting Season & Production: Early-season bearer (fruiting May through July in Florida) that yields heavy, consistent crops.
Disease Resistance:
Anthracnose: Exceptional resistance. Both the tree and the fruit hold up remarkably well against anthracnose fungus, making it a reliable performer in Florida's humid climate.
Bacterial Black Spot (BBS): Moderately susceptible, though rarely severe enough to cause major crop loss.
Pests/Dieback: Can occasionally be prone to tip dieback or attract scale pests, but it remains a top choice for Florida fruit growers seeking early citrus-flavored crops.


Kasar
The "Wow" Factor
The "Saffron" Mango: Named after kesar (saffron) due to its striking, vibrant deep saffron-orange flesh.
Florida Success Story: Known in India as the "Queen of Mangoes," it is one of the few classic Indian cultivars that translates exceptionally well to Florida's climate—yielding much better crops than other notoriously finicky varieties like 'Alphonso'.
Monoembryonic Indian Classic: Unlike Southeast Asian polyembryonic varieties, 'Kesar' produces a monoembryonic seed, carrying a rich, resinous spice lineage. However this seedling would need grafting of another Kasar to create fruit of that flavor.
Flavor & Texture Profile
Flavor: Intensely sweet and rich with a signature Indian spice profile—combining honey-like sweetness with complex, aromatic warm-spice notes. It has virtually no harsh tartness.
Texture: 100% fiberless, silky, and smooth with an exceptionally high flesh-to-seed recovery.
Appearance & Aroma: Small to medium fruit (0.5 to 1 lb) with greenish-yellow skin that develops a soft pinkish-red blush in direct sun. It is intensely aromatic when fully ripe.
Tree Characteristics & Disease Resistance
Growth Habit: A medium-sized tree with a moderately vigorous, spreading canopy. It responds well to annual pruning and can easily be maintained at 10 to 15 feet in home orchards.
Fruiting Season & Production: Mid-season bearer in Florida (harvesting late June through July) with reliable, consistent production.
Disease Resistance:
Anthracnose: High resistance. Handles Florida’s humid summer rains surprisingly well for an Indian cultivar.
Powdery Mildew: Susceptible. The blooms can be prone to powdery mildew during spring flowering, requiring monitoring or treatment during wet blooms.
Bacterial Black Spot (BBS): Moderately susceptible.


Sia Siam
The "Wow" Factor
A Pink-Blushed Thai Beauty: While presumably originating from Thailand (and closely related to cultivars like 'Maha Chanok'), 'Sia Siam' (or Si Siam) stands out visually with an oblong fruit shape that develops a gorgeous light red to pink blush over golden-yellow skin.
High Flesh-to-Seed Ratio: The fruit contains a remarkably thin, flat seed, giving you a maximum yield of edible flesh.
Off-Season Potential: Much like 'Choc Anon', it is known for its ability in warm microclimates to bloom in multiple waves and occasionally produce off-season crops in addition to its main summer harvest. Nom Doc Mai also can do this.
Flavor & Texture Profile
Flavor: A classic Indochinese/Thai honey-sweet flavor profile. It is pure, rich, and floral with deep honey undertones and virtually zero tartness or acid component.
Texture: Pale yellow, silky, and 100% fiberless with a smooth melt-in-your-mouth feel.
Appearance: Oblong, plump fruits typically borne in heavy clusters.
Tree Characteristics & Disease Resistance
Growth Habit & Yield: A highly productive, manageable tree that sets abundant crops.
Fruiting Season: Early-season bearer, typically maturing from June through July in Florida.
Disease Resistance:
Anthracnose: Exceptional resistance. It holds up remarkably well against Florida's humidity and rainy summer conditions, consistently yielding clean, spot-free fruit.
Overall Hardiness: Performs reliably in Florida landscapes with minimal spray maintenance required to keep fungal issues at bay.
Bailey’s Marvel

The "Wow" Factor
The "Upgraded" Haden: Originating on Pine Island in Bokeelia, Florida, 'Bailey's Marvel' is a chance seedling of 'Haden' (believed to be crossed with 'Bombay'). It delivers that rich, old-school Florida mango flavor people love, but fixes its parent's biggest flaw by being completely fiberless.
Impressive Fruit Size: Produces large, eye-catching fruit typically weighing between 1 to 2 pounds with a vibrant orange-red blush over a freckled yellow background.
Noteworthy Cold Hardiness: Highly regarded among Florida growers for possessing better cold tolerance than many other commercial varieties, enabling it to recover quickly from light frosts.
Flavor & Texture Profile
Flavor: Superb, classic-group flavor profile. It features a rich, sweet mango base balanced with a pleasant, tangy brightness and deep aromatic complexity.
Texture: Bright orange, firm, and silky smooth with zero fiber.
Aroma & Seed: Highly aromatic with a monoembryonic seed.
Tree Characteristics & Disease Resistance
Growth Habit: A vigorous, large tree that forms a dense, beautifully symmetrical, rounded canopy.
Fruiting Season: Early-to-mid season bearer (harvesting from June through August in Florida) with an extended ripening window.
Disease Resistance:
Bacterial Black Spot (BBS): Highly resistant.
Anthracnose/Fungal Issues: Moderate. It performs exceptionally well in coastal Florida microclimates. However, in humid inland areas, the dense canopy can trap moisture, making it somewhat susceptible to summer fungal spot. Regular canopy pruning to improve airflow helps keep the foliage and fruit clean.

Ugly Betty

The "Wow" Factor
Bumpy Exterior, Stellar Interior: Named for its unusually lumpy, uneven skin, 'Ugly Betty' proves that looks can be deceiving. It was bred in Boynton Beach, Florida by Gary Zill as a seedling of 'Carrie'.
Exceptional Flesh-to-Seed Ratio: Despite its small-to-medium size, it has a thin monoembryonic seed and "freestone" characteristics where the seed separates very cleanly from the pulp.
Striking Mature Color: While the skin is bumpy, it matures to a lovely pale yellow-green with a mottling of soft pink-to-red blush in direct sun.
Flavor & Texture Profile
Flavor: Heavy Indian-Alphonso flavor class. Inheriting much of its parentage from 'Carrie', it offers deep sweetness paired with rich spice notes and a touch of resin, but with less intense acidity.
Texture: Bright yellow-orange flesh that is 100% fiberless, extremely soft, and drippingly juicy.
Aroma: Intensely aromatic with a warm, spicy perfume.
Tree Characteristics & Disease Resistance
Growth Habit: A slow-to-moderate, upright vertical grower that tends to stay compact-to-medium in size.
Production: Generally considered a light-to-moderate, mid-season bearer (ripening primarily from late June through July).
Disease Resistance:
Anthracnose: Moderate tolerance. It has proven capable of producing clean fruit in more humid inland environments (such as Loxahatchee, FL) where other sensitive varieties fail.
Overall Hardiness: Requires minimal spraying compared to classic Indian varieties, though like most mangoes, it sets fuller crops in areas with good airflow.
Sindhiri
The "Wow" Factor
The National Pride of Pakistan: Known colloquially as the "Queen of Mangoes" in Pakistan, 'Sindhri' originates from the Sindh province (Mirpur Khas district) and is world-renowned for its colossal, elongated size and aromatic presence.
Eye-Catching "Golden Slipper" Shape: It produces very large, distinctively elongated and slightly curved fruit that ripens to a uniform, glowing golden-yellow with no red blush.
Extreme Honey Sweetness: It is famous for its remarkably high sugar levels and rich, nectar-like honey aroma that permeates an entire room when fully ripe.
Flavor & Texture Profile
Flavor: Intensely sweet and rich with a clean, honey-nectar profile and very mild, low acidity. It carries a pleasantly aromatic, tropical perfume without any heavy, resinous, or turpentine undertones.
Texture: The pale-yellow flesh is 100% fiberless, firm yet incredibly juicy, and melts cleanly in the mouth.
Seed Type: Produces a thin, elongated seed. In its native region, it is monoembryonic, though grafted trees are standard for nursery production.
Tree Characteristics & Disease Resistance
Growth Habit: A vigorous, large tree with a broad, spreading canopy and semi-erect branches. Because of its vigorous growth habit, it requires regular annual pruning in home landscapes to keep height manageable.
Fruiting Season: An early-to-mid season bearer (typically harvesting late May through June in native regions, extending into July in Florida microclimates).
Disease Resistance:
Anthracnose & Humidity: Moderate to Low. Because it evolved in the hot, dry, arid climate of the Sindh province, 'Sindhri' is somewhat sensitive to wet weather. During humid Florida summer rains, it can be prone to anthracnose fungal spotting if canopy airflow is restricted.
Management Tip: Performs best when planted in full sun with an open-pruned canopy to promote maximum air movement and keep foliage dry.
